Baby's First Year

A digital time capsule for baby's first year.

Every milestone, sealed until the candles. Grandparents, aunts, uncles, and close friends each drop in their own photos over the year — then everyone unlocks the whole story together on the first birthday.

Last updated — July 24, 2026

A baby's first year time capsule is a shared album that family and friends all add to across the first twelve months, sealed until the first birthday. Grandparents, siblings, aunts and friends contribute from their own phones, and nobody — including the parents who set it up — can open it until the year is finished.

The first year is the most photographed period of a person's life and the least organised. Photos sit across a dozen phones, in group chats that scroll away, and in messages nobody saved. A capsule collects them into one album while it's happening, rather than trying to reconstruct it later.

Why baby's first year

Family near and far, all in one place.

Not everyone who loves this baby lives close by. A first-year time capsule lets family members contribute their own moments as they happen, no matter the distance — and turns the first birthday into a real reveal, not just another photo dump.

  • – Grandparents and family near and far each drop in their own moments
  • – Nothing is visible early — not even to the parents, if you turn on Surprise mode
  • – Unlock together on the first birthday, or any date that means something to your family
  • – A year's worth of milestones, revealed all at once instead of trickling in one at a time
How it works

How does a first year time capsule work?

01

Start it when the baby arrives

Or before. Set the unlock for the first birthday, and an upload deadline a week or two ahead so the album is complete when it opens.

02

Everyone who visits adds

Grandparents, siblings, friends who came by with food. Each person adds from their own phone and sees nothing anyone else has added.

03

It opens on the birthday

The whole album arrives at once, on the day. Everyone who contributed gets the reveal at the same moment, including the relatives who only visited twice.

What should go in a baby’s first year time capsule?

Parents photograph the milestones without being told. What goes missing is everyone else's version of the year.

  • – Grandparents holding the baby — taken by someone else, so they’re actually in the frame
  • – The hospital bracelet, the going-home outfit, the car seat that took twenty minutes
  • – Siblings reacting, especially in the first week, especially unposed
  • – The 3am photos nobody would normally keep
  • – A short video from each visitor saying something to the one-year-old
  • – The same chair or blanket photographed monthly, so the size change is obvious
  • – The mess: the kitchen, the laundry, the state of the living room
  • – Whatever the baby was obsessed with that month and will never care about again

The relatives’ photos are the ones parents don’t have — because in almost all of them, the parents are holding the baby.

When should it unlock?

The first birthday is the obvious answer and the right one for a first capsule. It's a natural gathering, the album is exactly a year of material, and everyone who contributed is already thinking about the baby that day.

Each subsequent birthday works well as a repeating pattern — a new capsule per year, opened at the party. Groups can be set to create a fresh capsule automatically on a recurring schedule, so year two doesn't depend on anyone remembering to start it.

The eighteenth birthday is the version people find most affecting, and it needs the most thought. Seventeen years is long enough that the capsule needs to outlive several phones and at least one account migration — worth deciding deliberately rather than assuming.

Common questions

Who should be able to add to it?

Anyone you’d be happy having the photos. Members are invited individually or by link, the capsule is private to that group, and nothing is public at any point. A free capsule holds ten members; Pro holds fifty.

Can the parents look before the birthday?

Not if surprise mode is left on, which it is by default. It locks the person who created the capsule out along with everyone else. Plenty of parents deliberately turn it off at creation so they can see photos as they arrive — that’s a choice made once, when the capsule is set up.

What about relatives who aren’t good with apps?

They need the app to add photos, which is a genuine barrier for some grandparents. The lower-friction path is to have one person collect their photos and add them on their behalf — worth planning for rather than discovering at month eleven.

How much can one capsule hold?

Twenty photos on the free tier and a thousand on Pro. For a full year with several contributors, plan on needing the higher limit.
