Family Reunion

A shared photo album for family reunions.

Everyone's angle of the same weekend. Cousins, aunts, uncles, and grandparents all drop their photos into one private album. Then it opens for the whole family at once, whether that's this weekend or next year's reunion.

Last updated: July 24, 2026

A family reunion time capsule is one shared album that everybody at the reunion adds to, sealed until a date after everyone has gone home. Instead of photos ending up scattered across twenty phones and three group chats, they collect in one place, and nobody sees any of it, including whoever set it up, until it opens for the whole family at once.

Reunions have a particular failure mode: everyone photographs the weekend, a handful of pictures get sent round, and the rest are never seen by anyone outside the phone they're on. A capsule captures the other ninety percent, including from the relatives who never post anything.

Why family reunions

One album, every generation.

A reunion is the rare time the whole family is actually in one place, but everyone's photos end up scattered across a dozen phones and never seen again. A family reunion capsule collects them all in one spot, so the group can relive the whole weekend together, from every angle.

  • – Every relative adds their own photos: no one has to be the designated photographer
  • – Relatives who couldn't make it can still contribute old photos ahead of time
  • – Unlock the night everyone's still together, or wait until next year's reunion
  • – Vote on family superlatives when it opens: Life of the party, Best candid, and more
How it works

How does a family reunion time capsule work?

01

Set it up before people travel

Create it, share the link in the family group chat, and let people join on the way. Opening the link joins the capsule directly, which matters when half the family won’t troubleshoot an invite.

02

Everyone shoots their own weekend

The kitchen, the drive, the table, the kids. Nobody sees anyone else’s photos while the reunion is happening, so nobody edits themselves to match what’s already there.

03

It opens once everyone’s home

Set a date a week or two out, or set it to unlock only when the family is physically together again, which makes the next reunion the reveal.

What should go in a family reunion time capsule?

The posed group photo will happen with or without a capsule. Everything around it is what disappears.

  • – The arrivals: the driveway, the hugs, the first twenty minutes
  • – The kitchen, continuously, because that’s where the reunion actually takes place
  • – The failed attempts at the group photo, which are always better than the successful one
  • – The oldest and youngest person present, photographed together
  • – Someone telling a story, filmed for thirty seconds without asking permission first
  • – The house, the yard, the table mid-meal rather than before it
  • – Relatives who couldn’t travel, joining by video call (photographed off the screen)
  • – The goodbyes, which nobody ever photographs and everybody later wishes they had

Ask each household to add ten photos rather than asking the group to add photos. A specific number aimed at a specific person produces a dramatically fuller album.

When should a family reunion capsule unlock?

One to two weeks after everyone travels home is the default that works. The weekend has ended, the group chat has gone quiet, and the capsule arrives exactly when the reunion starts to feel over.

At the next reunion is the more ambitious option, and it's the one this format is genuinely best at. A capsule can be set to stay sealed until every member has checked in within roughly a hundred metres of each other, so it opens when the family is physically back in one place, however many months or years that takes. The reveal becomes part of the next gathering rather than an email nobody opens.

For families that reunite on a fixed schedule, a group can create a new capsule automatically each year, so it doesn't depend on the same one person organising it every time.

Common questions

Will relatives who aren’t technical actually manage this?

Joining is a single tap on a link. There’s no account approval step or invite code. Adding photos does require the app, though, which is the real barrier. The reliable pattern is one person per household who collects and uploads for everyone in it.

How many people can join?

Ten on a free capsule and fifty on Pro. Fifty covers most extended families; beyond that, splitting by branch tends to produce a better album anyway.

Can people who couldn’t attend still see it?

Yes, anyone invited to the capsule sees it when it opens, whether or not they were there. For relatives who couldn’t travel, being in the capsule is a much better version of the weekend than the handful of photos that would otherwise get texted to them.

What if someone adds a photo others don’t want in there?

Any member can report a photo, and the capsule owner can remove members. Because nothing is visible until the unlock date, though, this comes up far less than in a live shared album. There’s no audience to perform for while the capsule is filling.
