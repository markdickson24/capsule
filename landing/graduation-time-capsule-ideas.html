Graduation

Graduation time capsule ideas.

Lock it now. Open it when you've made it. Senior year moves fast — the last dorm hangout, the final home game, the summer before everyone scatters. A graduation time capsule holds all of it until you're ready to look back.

Last updated — July 24, 2026

A graduation time capsule is a shared album that a whole friend group fills during their final year, sealed until a date after everyone has scattered. Each person adds from their own phone, nobody can look early, and it opens for the entire group at the same moment — usually a year or five years out, when the group has stopped being in one place.

It solves a specific problem: senior year is heavily photographed and almost none of it is ever revisited. The photos live on twelve different phones, get deleted in storage cleanups, and are effectively gone within two years. A capsule pulls them into one album before that happens.

Why graduation

A capsule for the whole chapter.

Not just the highlight reel — the whole friend group's version of senior year, freshman year, or the four years in between. Everyone contributes their own photos and videos, and nobody sees anything until the group decides it's time.

  • – Start it early — freshman year, a big trip, or the first day of senior year

How it works

How does a graduation time capsule work?

01

Start it before the last semester

Not on graduation day. The best material is the ordinary weeks — the library at 2am, the walk everyone did daily — and that’s over by the time you’re in a cap and gown.

02

Everyone in the group adds

A capsule can hold up to fifty people, so it scales from a house to an entire class. Each person contributes from their own phone and nobody sees anyone else’s additions.

03

It opens at the reunion

Set it to unlock on a date, or only once everybody has physically checked in together — which turns the first time the group reassembles into the reveal itself.

Graduation time capsule ideas: what to put in it

The instinct is to fill it with graduation day. Graduation day is the most photographed and least interesting part of the year — everyone already has those pictures.

  • – The dorm room or house before it was packed up, filmed as a slow walkthrough
  • – The regular table — in the dining hall, the library, the bar — with whoever was at it
  • – A short clip of each person answering “where do you think you’ll be in five years?”
  • – The inside jokes, photographed literally, with no explanation for future-you
  • – The walk everyone did every single day and nobody ever photographed
  • – The professor, the shift manager, the person who ran the desk at night
  • – Last day of classes, in whatever undignified state that actually was
  • – The group’s failed attempts at a group photo, rather than the good one
  • – Voice notes recorded in a car at the end of a night out

A capsule filled with these is worth far more at a reunion than another set of cap-and-gown photos, which everyone’s parents already have.

When should a graduation time capsule unlock?

One year out is the sweet spot for a first capsule. It's long enough that everyone's life has visibly changed, and short enough that the group is still in touch by default rather than by effort.

Five years out is the version people describe as worth the wait, and the one that most often gets abandoned — five years is long enough for the group chat to go quiet and for nobody to remember the capsule exists. It works far better with a notification-based app than a shoebox.

At the first reunion, whenever that turns out to be, is the strongest option if the group already reunites. Rather than guessing a date, set the capsule to unlock only once every member has checked in within about a hundred metres of each other. It stays sealed for however long that takes — then opens in the room, with everyone there.

Common questions

How many people can be in one?

Up to ten members on a free capsule and fifty on a Pro one, which covers everything from a house to a whole graduating cohort. Everyone contributes from their own phone.

What if someone doesn’t add anything?

The capsule still opens on schedule for everyone else. Setting an upload deadline a few weeks before the unlock date, rather than the day before, collects noticeably more — most people contribute when reminded, not when invited.

Can we keep adding after graduation?

Yes, up until whatever upload deadline you set. Plenty of groups leave contributions open through the summer, which catches the move-out and the scattering — often the most affecting part of the album.

Is this better than a physical time capsule?

It’s better at surviving. A buried box depends on somebody remembering the location and still living near it in five years. The trade-off is real, though: a physical capsule holds objects, and a digital one only holds pictures of them.
