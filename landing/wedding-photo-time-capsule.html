Weddings

The wedding photo time capsule.

Every guest's photos, opened when the chaos settles. Instead of a camera roll nobody looks at again, your wedding photos come together in one place, sealed until the moment you choose to open them.

Last updated: July 24, 2026

A wedding photo time capsule is a shared album that every guest adds to during the wedding, sealed so that nobody (including the couple) can open it until a date you choose. Guests photograph the day from where they're standing: the table you never got to, the toast from three rows back, the dance floor at midnight. None of it appears anywhere until the capsule unlocks, and then it opens for everyone at the same moment.

It's deliberately unlike a shared album or a hashtag. There's no live feed to scroll, no drip of notifications through the reception, and nothing for the couple to accidentally see on the drive to the airport. The wait is the feature: you get one reveal instead of a hundred small ones.

Why weddings

Guests capture what the couple can't see.

The couple is at the center of their own wedding, which means they miss almost everything happening around them. A wedding photo time capsule collects every guest's angle in one private album, then reveals it all at once.

  • – Guests drop in photos and videos from their phones: no group text, no forgotten shared album
  • – Nobody can preview the album, not even the couple, until the unlock date arrives
  • – Unlock on the one-year anniversary, the morning after, or any date you choose
  • – Guests can keep adding right up until a contribution deadline you set
How it works

How does a wedding photo time capsule work?

01

Set it up before the day

Create the capsule, pick the unlock date, and set an upload deadline so late additions can still land after the honeymoon. Surprise mode is on by default, which locks the couple out too.

02

Guests add from their own phones

Share a link or QR code on the table cards. Opening the link joins the capsule, and there is no account approval step to slow anyone down mid-reception.

03

It opens for everyone at once

On the unlock date every guest gets the reveal simultaneously, then votes on awards like best photo of the night. Votes are anonymous and the tallies stay hidden until voting closes.

What should go in a wedding time capsule?

The professional photographer covers the wedding. The capsule should cover everything the photographer physically could not be standing next to.

  • – Getting-ready rooms, both of them, from whoever was actually in there
  • – The ceremony from the guest side, including the faces watching rather than the couple
  • – The cocktail hour conversations the couple was pulled away from
  • – Table-level candids: the grandparents, the kids under the table, the seating-chart chaos
  • – Toasts filmed from the audience, where you can hear the room react
  • – The dance floor after 10pm, which almost never survives on anyone’s camera roll
  • – Short video messages from guests, recorded at the table while they still had something to say
  • – The exit, the after-party, and whatever happened at 2am

A useful instruction on the table card: “photograph what you can see that the couple can’t.” It produces a far better album than “post your photos.”

When should a wedding time capsule unlock?

There are three unlock dates that work, and they produce genuinely different albums.

The morning after. The wedding is still happening emotionally, and you get the reveal over breakfast with whoever stayed. Best if you want the capsule to be part of the weekend rather than a separate event later.

Two to four weeks later. Long enough that you've forgotten the details, short enough that everyone still cares. This is the safest default, and it lands while guests will still open a notification about your wedding.

The first anniversary. The strongest version, and the hardest to wait for. A year is long enough that most of these photos will be genuinely new to you, because you never saw them and neither did anyone else. If you choose this, set an upload deadline a month after the wedding so the album closes while people still remember to contribute.

Common questions

How is this different from a shared album or a wedding hashtag?

A shared album is visible the moment someone uploads, so the photos trickle out over the following week and the moment is spent. A hashtag scatters them across a platform that will reorder and eventually bury them. A time capsule holds everything back and opens once, and because nothing is visible while it fills, nobody self-censors based on what has already been posted.

Can our photographer’s photos go in it?

They can, but the capsule is usually better without them. Professional galleries arrive weeks later and are already a curated set. The capsule is for the unpolished parallel record: the angles nobody was paid to cover.

What if guests forget to add anything?

Set an upload deadline rather than relying on the wedding day itself. Most contributions arrive in the two or three days afterwards, when people are going back through their camera rolls. A capsule that closes the same night collects far less than one that stays open for a week.

Can the couple really not see it early?

No. Surprise mode is on by default and locks the capsule’s creator out exactly like every other member. They can see how many photos are waiting, but not open any of them. There is no toggle to switch it off after the capsule exists, specifically so nobody can peek the night before.
